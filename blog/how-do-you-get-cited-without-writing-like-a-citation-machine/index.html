How do you get cited without writing like a citation machine?

By Daniel Rosenthal. Published September 6, 2026. Last updated September 6, 2026.

How do you get cited without writing like a citation machine?

You put the answer where a crawler can lift it, then you cut every sentence that exists only to announce the method. Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity cite pages that resolve a question in plain HTML. They also amplify whatever voice you put on the page. If every section opens with a question, dumps a two-sentence answer, then stamps a summary label, you trained them to quote a rubric, not a person.

Here is the tension. Extractable copy wins citations. Extractable copy that wears a uniform loses the reader, and then it loses the next citation too, because nobody links a page that sounds like homework.

Why do citation-friendly pages start to sound fake?

Because the cheap version of the pattern is a stamp. Question heading. Direct answer. Recap line. Next question. Recap line. The facts might be true. The rhythm is not. People can taste a microwave paragraph. They can also taste a page that was assembled for an answer engine and never read aloud.

I have read this pattern before, in a different decade. When a format works, the market floods it. Search once rewarded keyword density. Then it rewarded people who stuffed the page until it was unreadable. Answer engines will reward the same mistake if we let them: pages that are easy to quote and impossible to trust.

Why prompted copy still tastes like slop is the first failure mode, pasting the model live. This is the second: over-correcting until the page is a scoring sheet.

What does a crawler actually need?

The words in the first HTML response. A question the reader would type. An answer in the next sentences, not after a script runs. Schema in the head that matches those words. One job per URL. Prices and names in text, not in a picture.

It does not need a label that says the answer is coming. It does not need you to narrate that the copy was meant to sound human. If you have to print the test on the page, you already failed the test.

How do you keep a unique voice while still using an LLM? is the private-notes version of the same split. Long instructions stay off the live page. The visitor gets the cooking, not the kitchen.

How do you hold both sides at once?

Write for the person first, then check that a crawler can still steal a clean block. Keep the question headings if they are real questions. Keep the answer up front. Fold the recap into the last paragraph instead of a stamped summary. Cut the em dashes the model loves. Read the page out loud. If it sounds like a productized playbook, cut until it does not.

Then a person still has to ship it. The Large Language Model (LLM) will happily generate both the slop and the over-structured fix. Neither one is the page.
