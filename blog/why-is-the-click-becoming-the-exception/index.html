Why is the click becoming the exception?

By Daniel Rosenthal. Published September 6, 2026. Last updated September 6, 2026.

Gilad de Vries posted a screenshot of a Google results page and put the argument in the headline: the click is becoming the exception. He is co-founder and Chief Business Officer at Axioma. I am not restating his product. I am reading the page he showed.

The query is "best running shoes for marathon training." The first thing on the screen is an AI Overview. It writes a short brief on cushioning, support, and durability. It then lists four models with photos: Nike ZoomX Vaporfly Next% 3, Brooks Ghost 16, ASICS Gel-Nimbus 26, Saucony Endorphin Speed 4. Under that sits an Ask anything box. Runner's World, REI, and Verywell Fit are still there. They are lower.

Gilad marked the Overview plus the chat field as roughly 80 percent of the screen. I did not measure the pixels. I looked at the same layout. The organic list is no longer the first job the page does.

What does this screenshot actually show?

It shows Google finishing a commercial question without sending the reader to a publisher first. The Overview is the article. The product row is the merchandising. The Ask anything field is the next session, still on Google.

That is not a new theory. It is a layout. If you still plan media as if the first click is a blue link, you are planning against a page that no longer looks like that.

I have watched this pattern before. Television did not kill newspapers on day one. It moved the first sit-down. Search is doing the same thing to the open web, only the sit-down is now an answer box with four shoes and a prompt.

Why would a shopper stay on the results page?

Because the page already did the three jobs they used to farm out. It summarized the category. It named products. It offered a follow-up question without a new tab.

A person training for a marathon does not owe Runner's World a visit. They owe their knees a decision. If Google hands them four names and a chat field, many of them will stay. That is not a moral failure. It is a shorter path.

The publishers still appear. They appear as proof under the fold. Proof is not the same as traffic. If your revenue line is pageviews, this screenshot is a warning. If your revenue line is being the source the answer used, it is a different job.

Who loses the click, and who still gets paid?

The sites that used to win the query lose the first visit. The brands in the Overview still get named. Google still owns the session. Affiliate pages that lived on "best X for Y" lists lose the hop they used to charge for.

Gilad has asked this in public before, including around affiliate meetings: how do publishers get paid when the model makes the recommendation instead of the click? I will not pretend I have his contract. I will say the last-click story is the wrong story if the value was created when the answer picked the four shoes.

Ad tech that only prices a human click on a publisher page is measuring the leftover. The leftover is still real. It is no longer the whole market.

What should an ad in a conversation do instead?

It should show up after the person asked, labelled, and matched to that question. Not a banner parked under an answer they already finished. Not a cookie trail from last Tuesday.

That is the same shift I wrote about when I said the question is the ad. Behavioral targeting followed the user around the web. The stronger signal is the sentence they just typed. Native ads in a chat sit in that sentence. They are not a second search page glued to the side.

Google's Overview is Google doing that job for itself. If you run a smaller bot, you do not get that real estate. You get the thread you already have. Use it, or do not. Do not pretend a display strip under the widget is the same thing.

The demo on this site is scripted. There is not a live bid behind every visitor. Say that when you talk about inventory.

Does ranking still matter if nobody clicks?

Yes, if you mean being in the sources the Overview used. No, if you mean position one as a traffic plan.

Invisibility in the answer is worse than a lower link. The link is still there in Gilad's shot. It is just not where the eye starts. Citation work is how you make the live HTML easy to lift. It does not force Google to send the click. Anyone who sells you that is selling a story from 2014.

The trade-off is ugly and honest. You can write for the person who still clicks through. You can write so a model can quote you without lying. You should do both. You should not budget as if the click rate of a decade ago is coming back because you added schema.

Gilad put a picture on the table. The picture is enough. The click is becoming the exception on that query. Plan the money for the session that never leaves, and for the rare visit that still does.

A chat-ad path that is not ChatGPT-priced

Indie chatbot apps still need labelled partner offers. ChatGPT-scale ad deals are out of reach. The SDK returns a labelled card or nothing. Three lines. The key stays on your server.
